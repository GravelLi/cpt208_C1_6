About the project

What are we building?

Our project explores a digital platform for a university running club. The platform supports both regular student runners and club leaders by centralizing event information, improving social connection, and making group running safer and easier to manage.

The Why

Designing for active lives, wellbeing, and safer social running

Our group selected the Active track because movement is deeply connected to student wellbeing, confidence, and everyday routine. Running is not only a physical activity; for many university students it is also a way to manage stress, stay socially connected, and build healthy habits during a demanding academic period. At the same time, participation is shaped by practical concerns such as schedule coordination, confidence, visibility of events, and personal safety. These questions become even more important when we consider women runners, first-time participants, and students who want community but may feel uncertain about joining alone. HerRun responds to this space by exploring how a digital platform could make running feel more welcoming, better organized, and easier to trust. We are interested in the point where motivation, communication, and safety meet. By focusing on a university context, the project stays grounded in a real environment with recurring events, recognizable stakeholders, and strong opportunities to support both individual wellbeing and collective participation.

The Gap

Comparison across research papers and commercial products

We are comparing what existing academic and market solutions already support, and where they still leave unmet needs around motivation, safety, coordination, and social comfort.

Academic Papers

Structured notes for four references we will review in depth.

Paper 1

Did well
  • Explained motivation patterns among student runners.
  • Connected physical activity with social belonging.
  • Highlighted behavioural barriers in participation.
Missed
  • Did not address real-time coordination needs.
  • Offered little guidance on safety perception.
  • Lacked direct implications for product interaction.

Paper 2

Did well
  • Discussed community-based motivation in exercise.
  • Compared short-term and long-term engagement drivers.
  • Provided evidence for habit-forming support features.
Missed
  • Focused more on theory than actionable interface design.
  • Did not include club organizer perspectives.
  • Ignored onboarding anxiety for new members.

Paper 3

Did well
  • Examined trust and accountability in group activities.
  • Raised useful questions about communication clarity.
  • Suggested ways to reduce uncertainty before events.
Missed
  • Did not focus specifically on running contexts.
  • Overlooked emotional safety for women participants.
  • Provided limited comparison with digital tools.

Paper 4

Did well
  • Investigated wellbeing outcomes tied to active routines.
  • Made a strong case for accessible campus activities.
  • Supported the value of peer encouragement.
Missed
  • Did not explore scheduling and announcement friction.
  • Left organizer workflows underdeveloped.
  • Did not map how students discover events.
Commercial Products

Snapshot placeholders for four products we will compare against.

Product 1

Did well
  • Offered clean event discovery and scheduling tools.
  • Presented strong visual motivation through tracking.
  • Made progress and participation feel visible.
Missed
  • Weak support for group safety expectations.
  • Limited features for campus-specific communities.
  • Did not help organizers manage communication well.

Product 2

Did well
  • Included strong social sharing and interaction cues.
  • Encouraged routine participation through reminders.
  • Reduced friction in joining visible activities.
Missed
  • Did not distinguish needs of new and experienced runners.
  • Safety information was not foregrounded.
  • Club administration tools were minimal.

Product 3

Did well
  • Supported route planning and activity coordination.
  • Made participation logistics easier to understand.
  • Provided strong utility for recurring activities.
Missed
  • Visual design felt task-heavy rather than welcoming.
  • Social belonging was under-emphasized.
  • Motivation features lacked emotional resonance.

Product 4

Did well
  • Created trust through clear status and update patterns.
  • Handled notifications and reminders effectively.
  • Showed how structured information can reduce confusion.
Missed
  • Did not address emotional hesitation before joining.
  • Offered weak support for inclusive community tone.
  • Lacked a tailored experience for university runners.

Requirements List

Three must-have conditions for a playful HerRun experience

These placeholder requirements define the minimum conditions the system should meet to feel approachable, socially engaging, and emotionally rewarding.

Must-Have 1

Encourage low-pressure engagement

The experience should help students join without feeling judged, overcommitted, or overwhelmed before they feel ready.

Must-Have 2

Support playful social interaction

The system should make communication, participation, and shared progress feel light, welcoming, and socially motivating.

Must-Have 3

Provide emotionally positive feedback

The platform should reward effort with encouraging cues, visible progress, and small moments of delight that strengthen confidence.
