Motivation & Research

Why this direction matters and where design opportunity appears

This page frames the value of HerRun through motivation, comparison, and stakeholder understanding. It establishes why the project matters in the first place before later pages move into requirements, ideas, and implementation.

The Why

Designing a Women-Centred Running System for Wellbeing and Support

We chose the Active track because more people today are paying attention to both physical health and mental well-being. In a fast-paced lifestyle, exercise is not only a way to improve fitness and body condition, but also an important way to reduce stress, regulate emotions, and gain positive psychological feedback. It has become an essential part of daily health management for many people.

However, we found that most existing fitness products still focus mainly on physical indicators, such as heart rate, calories burned, pace, and workout duration. These metrics are useful, but they do not fully reflect the user’s overall experience. In particular, they often ignore psychological factors such as mood changes, motivation, stress level, and emotional recovery after exercise.

We believe this gap creates an opportunity for innovation. By combining physical data with mental health feedback, a product can help users better understand their overall condition and encourage them to build healthier, more sustainable exercise habits. This approach makes exercise feel not only measurable, but also supportive and meaningful.

For these reasons, we believe the Active track is the most suitable choice for our project.

The Gap

Comparison across academic papers and commercial products

These placeholders show the structure we will use to compare four academic references and four commercial products, focusing on what each one supports well and what it still misses.

Commercial Products

Four product comparisons for running and fitness support.

Product 1: Nike Run Club

Did well
  • Strong guided running experience.Audio-Guided Runs give real-time coaching, especially useful for beginners and solo runners.
  • Motivates through challenges and community.Weekly, monthly, custom, friend, and global challenges help users stay engaged.
  • Includes location sharing for safety.Real-time sharing with friends or family supports both connection and runner safety.
Missed
  • Mood reflection is not central.NRC tracks and coaches runs, but does not clearly address mental state before or after exercise.
  • Feels too broad for casual local running.Global communities and challenges do not fully support finding a nearby friend for a relaxed run.
  • Women-specific support is not a visible core feature.The app focuses on structured coaching and training, with less attention to low-intensity, low-stress needs.

Product 2: Strava

Did well
  • Strong social fitness ecosystem.Share, connect, clubs, challenges, and race courses make exercise feel socially active.
  • Powerful tracking and route analysis.Routes, maps, segments, pace, distance, and performance tools are mature for detailed runners.
  • Beacon supports safer solo activity.Safety contacts can view a live activity link with frequent location updates.
Missed
  • Performance and competition dominate.Segments and leaderboards may feel stressful for users seeking low-pressure running.
  • Emotional wellbeing is not a key feature.The app centers tracking, sharing, exploring, and competing rather than mood check-ins or reflection.
  • Advanced tools require subscription.Routes, segment leaderboards, and deeper training analysis may be less accessible for budget-conscious students.

Product 3: Keep

Did well
  • Broad fitness ecosystem beyond running.Keep covers fitness, running, yoga, step counting, and training plans for varied exercise goals.
  • Strong guided training content.Coaching videos and personalized recommendations make it approachable for beginners.
  • Combines community with nearby runners.Users can share workouts, track activity, and discover nearby joggers through GPS.
Missed
  • Broad, but not women-centred.Its core structure does not clearly focus on emotion, safety, cycle awareness, or low-stress group running.
  • Mood reflection is not integrated.Pre-run and post-run mood, emotional change, and running advice are not central to the running flow.
  • Can feel too feature-heavy.The one-stop sports model may burden casual runners who mainly want relaxation and stress relief.

Product 4: Gudong

Did well
  • Broad sports tracking ecosystem.Gudong covers running, cycling, walking, hiking, fitness, and more with GPS and sensor-based data analysis.
  • Connects running with events.Its marathon platform supports event types from 5K and 10K to half and full marathons.
  • Supports smart devices and AI training.Watch binding, heart rate monitoring, voice guidance, and Pace Rabbit support structured pace training.
Missed
  • Too broad for casual wellbeing runners.The wide scope may weaken focus for users seeking low-pressure running and lightweight health support.
  • Advanced support depends on membership.Personalized plans, AI tools, voice coaching, deeper analysis, and course support may feel less accessible to students.
  • AI is performance-oriented.Pace Rabbit focuses on pace control and marathon improvement rather than everyday emotional wellbeing.

Academic Papers

Four structured academic references for literature comparison.

Paper 1: Motivations, barriers and exercise preferences among female undergraduates: A need assessment analysis.

Did well
  • Used both questionnaires and interviews.This gave participants a safer space to explain their exercise perceptions, motivations, and preferences.
  • Covered motivation, barriers, and preferences together.The study summarized eight motivation themes and five barrier themes, giving a fuller view of what supports or prevents exercise.
  • Connected findings to practical design.It translated the need for structured but flexible plans into an exercise module.
Missed
  • Used a narrow health-program sample.Participants may already understand exercise benefits better than the wider female undergraduate population.
  • Depended on one urban campus context.Traffic, commuting, and campus routines may shape activity barriers in ways that are not widely representative.
  • Excluded disability and mental health needs.This clarified the scope, but reduced insight into accessibility, wellbeing, and inclusive support.

Paper 2: Analysis of Self Efficacy and Social Physique Anxiety between women engaged in physical exercise and those having sedentary lifestyle

Did well
  • Compared two clearly defined lifestyles.The exercise and sedentary groups made differences in self-efficacy and social physique anxiety easier to examine.
  • Used established psychological scales.The Exercise Self-Efficacy Scale and SPAS gave the study a clearer research basis than general questions.
  • Reported statistical group differences.The study used independent sample t-tests with mean, standard deviation, t value, and p value results.
Missed
  • Presented inconsistent self-efficacy findings.The abstract and discussion conflict with the result table about which group scored higher.
  • Could not explain causal direction.The study shows relationships, but cannot prove whether exercise changes self-efficacy or anxiety.
  • Relied only on self-reported data.Subjective responses and social desirability bias may have affected the results.

Paper 3: Enhancing Mental Health, Well-Being and Active Lifestyles of University Students by Means of Physical Activity and Exercise Research Programs

Did well
  • Used a broad multi-method design.It combined psychological measures, activity assessment, tasks, and physiological indicators such as heart rate.
  • Compared exercise types and intensities.The studies examined cardio, cognitive, balance, and coordination activities instead of treating exercise as one general category.
  • Linked results to student life.Home, work, and classroom exercises, plus videos and bilingual manuals, helped reduce practical barriers.
Missed
  • Still needed stronger evidence.The authors note that more combined psychological and physiological research is required.
  • Included small or uneven samples.Several studies had limited participants, missing data, or dropouts, reducing stability and generalizability.
  • Focused on a restricted population.Eligibility rules controlled variables, but limited relevance to broader student groups.

Paper 4: Barriers and facilitators to physical activity for young adult women: a systematic review and thematic synthesis of qualitative literature

Did well
  • Matched the review design to the research question.The systematic review and thematic synthesis followed PRISMA and ENTREQ, with a PROSPERO protocol.
  • Used a social-ecological framework.Barriers were organized across individual, interpersonal, organizational, community, and environmental levels.
  • Highlighted women-specific factors.The review captured body image, family roles, culture, safety, and fear more directly than generic activity research.
Missed
  • Could not compare subgroups systematically.Incomplete source data limited analysis across location, occupation, income, education, and activity level.
  • Used restrictive inclusion criteria.English-language studies of healthy women aged 18-40 excluded pregnancy and postnatal contexts.
  • Depended on uneven study quality.CASP assessment found issues around ethics, researcher bias, and insufficient data in some included studies.

From Gap to Design

The research gap becomes a design response

The comparison above revealed a repeated gap: existing tools support tracking and training well, but give less attention to mood, low-pressure participation, women-centred safety, and supportive social connection.

01

Mood-aware support

Academic papers showed that exercise participation is shaped by motivation, confidence, stress, and emotional recovery. HerRun therefore treats mood as part of the running experience rather than only tracking physical performance.

02

Low-pressure participation

Commercial products such as NRC, Strava, Keep, and Gudong offer strong training, tracking, and challenge systems, but many of them still emphasize performance, competition, or complex feature sets. HerRun responds by making running feel lighter, more flexible, and less judgmental.

03

Women-centred safety and trust

Research and personas both highlighted the importance of safety, confidence, and comfort for women runners. HerRun therefore prioritizes safer solo running, supportive group participation, and health-aware design cues.

04

Supportive social connection

Personas helped translate broad research findings into concrete needs for companionship, event visibility, and organizer coordination. HerRun uses social connection as emotional support, not as a leaderboard-driven pressure system.
