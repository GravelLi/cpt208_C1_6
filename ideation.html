Design Alternatives

Comparing possible directions before selecting one

Here we show how we can document different concepts and explain why one direction was ultimately chosen for HerRun.

Alternative A

Ranking-Based Competition System

The system centers on performance metrics: leaderboards, personal records, pace comparisons, and weekly mileage rankings against other users. This approach is proven effective for motivation in general fitness apps (e.g. Strava, Nike Run Club) and provides clear, measurable progress indicators. However, our interview and questionnaire data revealed that female runners frequently cited competition and body-image pressure as barriers to running, not motivators. A ranking-first system would replicate the exact dynamic our target users are trying to escape, making it fundamentally misaligned with HerRun's purpose.

Alternative B

Route Planning System

The system focuses on GPS-guided route discovery, generating scenic or safe paths based on location, distance preference, and time of day. This directly addresses the safety concern raised across all five of our interviews. However, route planning alone treats running as a logistical problem rather than an emotional one. It does not account for days when a user's mood or physical condition makes any running feel inaccessible — which our research identified as the primary drop-off point for casual female runners.

Chosen Direction

Emotion-Centered System (Chosen)

The system places mood, recovery, and menstrual cycle awareness at the core of every interaction. Rather than asking "how far did you run?", it asks "how are you feeling today?" and adapts goals, language, and rewards accordingly. This design absorbs the strengths of both alternatives — safety-aware route options and visible progress — while shifting the primary frame from performance to wellbeing. It is the only approach that directly addresses the self-efficacy and emotional barriers our research consistently identified as the root cause of dropout among the target users.
